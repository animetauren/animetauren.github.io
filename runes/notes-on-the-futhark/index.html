notes on the futhark

The Elder Futhark is the oldest of the runic alphabets: twenty-four signs, each cut from straight lines, because straight lines are what a knife does well in wood.

That constraint is the interesting part. No curves, no shading, nothing that would need a steadier hand than a carver has. What's left is a system of intersections — vertical staves with branches leaning off them — and the branches carry the meaning.

I keep a list of them, mostly as a reference for myself. Fehu, uruz, thurisaz, ansuz. They read like a chant, which is fitting: the names are older than the letters, and the letters were never only sounds.

There's a lesson in that for anyone who works with type. The tool shapes the letter. A chisel gives you straight lines. A screen at fourteen pixels gives you something else entirely.
