on writing small things

A small thing is finished. That is the whole of the argument, and it is not a small argument.

Most software is never finished. It accumulates — a feature, an integration, a migration, another service to keep alive at three in the morning. Nobody decided this. It just happened, the way a room fills with paper.

So I've started asking a different question before I begin: what is the smallest version of this that is still honest? Not the smallest version that demos well, or the one that survives a planning meeting. The one where every part is earning its place.

Small things have a particular dignity. You can hold the whole of them in your head. You can explain them in a sentence. When they break, you know where to look.

The trade is real. Small things don't scale, don't impress, don't come with a roadmap. That's fine. Not everything needs one.
